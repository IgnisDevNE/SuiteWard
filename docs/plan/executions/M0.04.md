## What the scenarios establish

- Promotion and first-test bootstrap contenders load the same authority and reach controlled commit barriers; exactly one commits its version, pointer, history, receipt, audit, scheduling change and publication intent.
- Revocation before promotion invalidates the stale commit, and fresh evaluation blocks. Promotion before revocation invalidates the stale revoke; explicit reconciliation records the exact historical version without rewriting canonical history or scheduling.
- Policy, proposal, priority-transfer, waiting-queue and consent-alias changes advance the whole-authority fence. An alias or waiting-only change can leave both canonical ID and active scheduling generation unchanged and still invalidate an earlier decision.
- Command source aliases preserve original outcomes and reserve the new operation identity without duplicating audit or acknowledgment. Existing identities are read before missing or changed current authority; receipt-only snapshots cannot fabricate current authority.
- A merged but unpromotable active proposal retains its queue position. No timeout, automatic reassignment or remote check-demotion policy is introduced.
- Corrective promotion requires fresh consent and exact source-bound evidence, produces a new logical version with explicit historical attribution, and preserves old versions and records. Revocation during its commit race forces fresh reconciliation.
- Failure injection at version, pointer, promotion, receipt, consent, alias, audit, scheduling, publication and acknowledgment stages discards every staged effect. An external delivery failure is simulated separately: the committed publication intent remains available, and replay does not create another one.

Root independently reproduced each original cycle and reviewed final integration scenarios. A and B also reviewed each other's final source/tests. Supplemental scenarios characterize and compose existing behavior; they are not mislabeled as new pre-implementation failures.
